# Gameorilla forever log

**Status:** active design standard  
**Last updated:** July 25, 2026  
**Applies to:** Gameorilla, the ape vice arkade, every game, support surface, social card, sound cue, and future campaign.

This is the permanent decision record for Gameorilla. It combines the supplied Gameorilla brand kit with the approved direction: clean, original console-era arcade clarity filtered through Gameorilla's neon Vice world.

## One-sentence test

Gameorilla should feel like an original arcade title screen you want to press start on: simple, bold, playable, neon, and unmistakably ape-led.

## Source material and precedence

1. The five supplied brand-kit pages were reviewed and compiled into this log. Their non-negotiable decisions are recorded below so this repository remains self-contained even when the original presentation images are unavailable.
2. The supplied waterfront title artwork is fixed equity. Preserve the outside skyline and palms, shoreline, water, reflections, proportions, and pixel structure. The circle behind Mack Rilla uses the supplied clean striped portrait with no palms or buildings inside it. The canonical white O treatment is recorded below.
3. This log governs future product decisions. If a choice conflicts with the kit, preserve the supplied kit and record the new decision here.

## Approved design filter

Use original console-era arcade grammar, not a generic retro effect.

- One dominant focal point: title art, a game board, or one decisive action.
- A readable status strip: score, bananas, player count, level, or room state.
- A compact action menu with a visibly selected item and plain verbs.
- Brief character, shop, host, or situation pop-ups that tell the player what to do next, then get out of the way.
- Hard-edged frames, platforms, ladders, rails, or cabinet borders that clarify hierarchy.
- A small cast of bold, legible objects. Let empty black space work.
- Pixel geometry, stepped bands, and short looping motion—not glossy gradients, soft cards, or dashboard chrome.

This is inspiration from an era's interaction grammar, not a license to copy any company's characters, names, art, sounds, logos, level layouts, or typography. Everything shipped remains original Gameorilla work.

## Fixed identity

### World

Gameorilla is the ape vice arkade: a neon after-hours city of social games, palms, skyline, water, music, cool apes, and bananas. It is grown-up and playful—not aggressive, explicit, childlike, or casino-like.

### Mascot and logo

- Controller-eye glasses are the signature mascot device.
- Use the supplied full logo/title art intact whenever it is the hero asset.
- Use the supplied mark at its natural pixel proportions.
- Never make a photorealistic ape, smooth-vector remake, or different controller-eye shape.

### Canonical GAMEORILLA wordmark

GAMEORILLA is always one continuous word in one font, size, weight, baseline, and spacing system. The O is the transition between GAME and RILLA, but it never separates from the word or becomes a badge.

- GAME = color A.
- O = Pixel White (#F3F6FF) in the canonical GAMEORILLA title wordmark.
- RILLA = color B.
- Use color only to distinguish the O: no gap, italics, outline trick, size change, rotation, alternate font, or independent motion.
- The shoreline reflection mirrors the same three-color split.
- Uppercase GAMEORILLA is approved for the title card; lowercase gameorilla is approved for compact utility marks. The same O rule applies to both.

### Mack Rilla: the house gorilla

Mack Rilla is Gameorilla's named host and merch anchor. His face, controller-eye glasses, relaxed expression, and silhouette stay static and instantly recognizable. The world around him does the moving: broad stepped stripes, color bands, score flashes, simple platform geometry, and occasional neon scene changes.

- Default Mack Rilla asset: static face over a small set of bold horizontal stripes or a single-color field.
- Merch rule: the face must work in one color, two colors, or the full Vice palette without extra scenery.
- Hero rule: use the supplied waterfront title art edge to edge. Mask the original moon completely, then place the supplied clean striped Mack Rilla portrait as the only visible circle with clear vertical separation above the shoreline wordmark. Every non-logo opening in the upper title field continues the pixel star field; the entire water field below GAMEORILLA uses the untouched original waterfront water and reflections across the full width; never place a synthetic center panel, gradient mask, or alternate ripple texture over it. Stars and the outside cityscape remain the background; no second ring, flat black patch, or circle may remain. Apply the documented white O and place “the ape vice arkade” in Pixel White inside the reflected-water field close beneath GAMEORILLA.
- Transition rule: Mack may leave the badge only for short, original pixel vignettes—a top-down 1960s convertible cruise, a diner fly-in, a rooftop DJ set, or a winner celebration. The scene stays clean: one action, one vehicle or object, one joke, then back to play.
- Character rule: Mack is cool, helpful, and unbothered. He does not carry weapons or threaten people; a crossing paddle, hazard flag, or flashing sign carries a helpful street-crossing gag without changing his tone.

## Screen recipes

### Home and game select

1. Score/status strip at the top.
2. One framed title or game scene in the center.
3. One compact start menu with an unmistakable selected action.
4. A short PRESS START or direct action prompt.
5. A small bottom strip for the current cabinet, target, or instruction.

The live home screen is the canonical example. Its locked title-card order inside one picture is: POUNDTOWN GAMES PRESENTS / complete Mack Rilla circle / GAMEORILLA / the ape vice arkade. Mack's face and striped circle must not be covered by the wordmark. Outside and immediately below that picture, the approved two-line copy is “YOUR NEON AFTER-HOURS PARTY PRIMATE.” / “BRING YOUR CREW. STACK BANANAS. GO APE S***.” Do not repeat the tagline there.

### Pop-ups and micro-scenes

Use pop-ups as tiny social beats: a host leans in, a baddie at the bar has a challenge, a vendor has a cosmetic, or a rival has the next move. They make the arcade feel inhabited without turning a party game into a long story.

- One character or object, one short message, one clear next action.
- Use a hard-edged dialogue frame, nameplate, portrait/mark, and one or two choices at most.
- Keep it conversational and direct: YO, CREW. PICK A STACK. / THE DJ WANTS A WINNER. VOTE NOW. / NICE PLAY. RUN IT BACK?
- Use them for success, unlocks, and meaningful state changes—never to bury a required action.
- Dismiss with one button, PRESS START, or an obvious selection. Respect reduced motion and never trap keyboard focus.

### Information, support, policy, and account screens

These can be calmer, but never become white rounded corporate product pages. Keep the midnight field, hard borders, pixel type, a clear header/status strip, and sharp cabinet-like information panels.

### Game screens

- Require 3–8 active players before round one; shared gameboard devices do not count as players.
- Put round, score, timer, and turn state in a fast-scanning HUD.
- Show one active decision at a time.
- Use short instructions: WRITE, VOTE, WAIT, NEXT ROUND, or REMATCH.
- Reserve motion for state changes, success, errors, and time pressure.

## Language

Use short, active, human lines. The player should understand a screen in one breath.

| Use | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Start game | Begin your interactive experience |
| Pick a stack | Configure scoring parameters |
| Join crew | Complete participant onboarding |
| Vote loud | Submit your preference |
| Banana target | Points objective |
| Need a hand? | Contact our support department |

Casual wording—crew, stack, vice, peel, bananas—must stay welcoming. Never lean on threats, insults, slurs, gambling language, or performative toughness.

## Motion and sound

- Motion is functional: blink a start prompt, step a selection cursor, pulse a timer, lift a reward, or transition a round.
- Use short, discrete movement; no syrupy spring motion.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.
- Canonical DNA: 8-bit + 16-bit, FM synth leads, PSG pulse/square voices, triangle or sub-bass support, noise-channel percussion, and short PCM hits where needed.
- Overall feel: cool, playful, nocturnal, slightly edgy.
- UI/navigation: 80–200 ms, one to three notes, clean attack, high clarity.
- Success/reward: 300–900 ms, major intervals, rising contour, layered sparkle.
- Warning/error: 120–400 ms, minor second or tritone, downward contour, dry and direct.
- Theme music: 30–90 second loop, 100–140 BPM, hook in the first eight bars, with A/B loop sections.
- Do use arpeggios, syncopated bass, memorable motifs, and arcade energy.
- Avoid cinematic orchestra, sleepy lo-fi, modern EDM drops, and muddy mixes.

## No list

- No smooth modern illustration, generic SaaS cards, glassmorphism, beige, earth tones, or rounded-pill UI as the default.
- No photorealistic mascot art, copied game characters, copied layouts, copied music, copied title typography, or another brand's name in product copy.
- No childlike toy energy and no violent, hostile, or real-money-gambling cues.
- No long promotional paragraphs when a title, HUD label, or menu item will do.
- No altered Gameorilla logo art beyond the documented white O and clean striped Mack Rilla moon treatment.

## Build decision tree

1. What is the one action? Make it the selected menu item or biggest button.
2. What state must a player scan? Put it in a compact HUD/status strip.
3. Can the screen be read in three seconds? If not, remove copy or objects.
4. Does the layout use original cabinet/platform grammar? Add a rail, frame, or grid only when it clarifies hierarchy.
5. Are the accents disciplined? One dominant accent plus one support accent.
6. Would it read as Gameorilla without a text label? It should carry midnight black, neon structure, pixel intent, and ape-vice energy.
7. Could anyone mistake it for another company's game? If yes, redesign before review.
